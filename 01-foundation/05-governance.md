# F5 — Architecture governance

## Outcomes

Explain governance, the Architecture Board, compliance, contracts, capability, and the Enterprise Continuum/Repository distinction.

> **See it in practice:** Trace [an observability principle through an approved exception](../examples/foundation/05-governance-and-content.md).

**Governance** supplies decision rights, accountability, transparency, and compliance. **Management** executes within that system. Good governance accelerates routine decisions and makes exceptions explicit.

| Element | Role |
|---|---|
| Architecture Board | Oversees implementation of strategy through architecture; makes or governs key decisions |
| Architecture principles | Stable guidance for consistent decisions |
| Architecture Contract | Joint agreement on deliverables, quality, and responsibilities |
| Compliance review | Assesses implementation against architecture, requirements, and approved deviations |
| Architecture capability | People, roles, skills, processes, governance, and tools that perform EA |
| Architecture Repository | Stores outputs, standards, reference material, governance records, and landscape information |
| Enterprise Continuum | A way to classify and communicate assets from generic to organization-specific |

The Continuum is a **classification view**, not merely a database. The Repository is where relevant assets and records are organized and retained.

## Exception path example

A delivery team cannot meet the preferred integration standard. It documents impact, alternatives, risk, cost, and expiry; the authorized body accepts, rejects, or conditions the exception; the decision and technical debt are recorded; the exception is reviewed later. Quiet non-compliance is never an exception process.

## Hands-on

Create a five-row RACI for principle approval, architecture approval, standards exception, compliance review, and change trigger. Include sponsor, Architecture Board, lead architect, delivery lead, and operations.

## Knowledge check

1. Classification mechanism or storage location: which is the Enterprise Continuum? 2. What makes a waiver governable? 3. Why use an Architecture Contract?

**Answers:** 1. Classification mechanism. 2. Explicit authority, evidence, risk acceptance, conditions/expiry, and a recorded decision. 3. To align responsibilities, deliverables, and measures between parties.
