# F2 — ADM fundamentals

## Outcomes

You will be able to state the purpose of every ADM phase, explain Requirements Management’s central role, and distinguish objectives, activities, and outputs.

> **See it in practice:** Follow [one customer promise through every ADM phase](../examples/foundation/02-adm-walkthrough.md).

## The cycle as a story

| Part | Memory question | Typical intent |
|---|---|---|
| Preliminary | Are we ready and how will we work? | Establish capability; tailor framework; define principles/governance |
| Phase A: Architecture Vision | Why act, for whom, and within what scope? | Secure approval for a concise, shared direction |
| Phase B: Business Architecture | How should the business operate? | Develop Baseline/Target and business gaps |
| Phase C: Information Systems Architectures | What data and applications are needed? | Develop Data/Application Baseline, Target, gaps |
| Phase D: Technology Architecture | What technology supports the solution? | Develop Technology Baseline, Target, gaps |
| Phase E: Opportunities & Solutions | How can change be grouped? | Identify major implementation projects/work packages; outline roadmap |
| Phase F: Migration Planning | In what prioritized sequence? | Complete Implementation and Migration Plan; assess value/risk |
| Phase G: Implementation Governance | Is delivery conforming? | Govern implementation and Architecture Contract |
| Phase H: Architecture Change Management | Is the architecture still fit? | Monitor change and trigger appropriate new work |
| Requirements Management | What must be satisfied throughout? | Identify, store, communicate, and dispose of requirements |

The graphic is often drawn as a cycle, not a waterfall. Iteration may occur within a phase, between phases, or across cycles. Requirements flow into and out of every phase.

## Common distinctions

- Preliminary configures the **capability**; Phase A authorizes a particular **cycle**.
- E groups solution directions; F makes a detailed, prioritized migration plan.
- G governs implementation; H governs change to the architecture after and during evolution.
- Requirements Management is continuous, not a single numbered phase.

## Knowledge check

1. Where is readiness established? 2. Which phase turns grouped work into a prioritized plan? 3. Which phase handles a request caused by new regulation?

**Answers:** 1. Preliminary. 2. F. 3. H assesses it and determines the response; requirements remain managed centrally.
