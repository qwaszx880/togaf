# F4 — Applying the ADM

## Outcomes

Explain tailoring, iteration, levels, partitioning, and how the ADM can coexist with other delivery approaches.

> **See it in practice:** Compare [a regulated bank, a retailer, and a small charity](../examples/foundation/04-tailoring.md) to see why valid tailoring differs.

## Tailor deliberately

Tailoring adapts method and content to context without removing necessary control. Consider organizational model, governance, industry constraints, architecture maturity, scope, and integration with project/product methods. Record the rationale so “tailored” does not become “informal and unrepeatable.”

## Three useful iteration patterns

| Pattern | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Between ADM cycles | Evolve the enterprise over time | Annual strategy refresh triggers a new cycle |
| Between phases | Reconcile dependent domain findings | Technology constraint sends work back to Application Architecture |
| Within a phase | Refine from broad to detailed | Two Business Architecture passes deepen the operating model |

Architecture can be developed at strategic, segment, and capability levels. Work at these levels must be aligned rather than treated as isolated documents. **Partitioning** divides a complex architecture space while preserving coordination, ownership, and integration.

## Agile and ADM

ADM is not a software delivery lifecycle. It can establish guardrails, intentional architecture, and decision cadence while delivery teams learn incrementally. Feedback can update assumptions and requirements. The key is traceable decisions and sufficient governance—not excessive documents.

## Hands-on: tailor responsibly

A regulated bank has product teams releasing weekly. Define a minimal ADM cadence, three non-negotiable governance controls, and two feedback events. State what you omitted and why.

## Knowledge check

1. Does tailoring mean phases can be ignored silently? 2. Why partition? 3. What protects alignment during iterative delivery?

**Answers:** 1. No; adaptations need explicit rationale and required outcomes/control must remain. 2. To manage complexity and ownership while coordinating the whole. 3. Agreed principles, requirements, traceability, guardrails, reviews, and feedback.
