# Practitioner example: turn gaps into governed delivery

[Back to theory: Implementing architecture](../../02-practitioner/05-implementation.md)

## Situation

Gap analysis produces 18 gaps. The program manager proposes 18 parallel projects. Capacity supports only four teams.

## Phase E: consolidate by outcome

| Work package | Gaps consolidated | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Data foundation | semantics, ownership, quality, regional mapping | trustworthy available-to-reserve information |
| Reservation capability | lifecycle, interfaces, notification, identity | controlled reservation experience |
| Store operating model | roles, process, training, exceptions | adoptable fulfillment |
| Service assurance | measures, logs, alerts, support ownership | observable reliable service |

One gap is not automatically one project. Grouping exposes coherent delivery and shared dependencies.

## Phase F: sequence using evidence

1. Prove semantics and event feasibility; without this, service estimates are unreliable.
2. Build a thin end-to-end path with store co-design and assurance.
3. Pilot in ten diverse stores and collect outcome/readiness evidence.
4. Remediate and deploy in waves to the remaining 50.

The visible customer app is not first merely because executives like it. Priority balances value, dependencies, cost, risk, and readiness.

## Phase G: govern without managing the sprint

Before pilot, the team demonstrates requirement traceability, privacy controls, performance evidence, operational ownership, and approved deviations. The architect assesses conformance and advises; the delivery manager controls sprint tasks. A contract records responsibilities and evidence.

## Try it first

A team proposes a different event broker that meets all required capability, standards, cost, and risk. Is it non-conformant because the picture used another product?

**Debrief:** check whether the approved architecture mandated that Solution Building Block and why. If only capability/constraints were approved, the alternative may conform. Govern intent and requirements, not diagram aesthetics.

## Learner variation

Pilot results show excellent performance but 30% staff adoption. Phase G captures conformance and evidence; roadmap and readiness actions must change. Technical success does not equal outcome realization.
