# Worked example: vision and scope

## Problem and outcome

Customers cannot trust online availability, while stores improvise reservations. The target experience lets a customer reserve available stock, receive confirmation within two minutes, collect through a consistent process, and receive clear exception communication. Success is cancellation below 4% in the pilot, adoption above 85%, controlled privacy risk, and no material peak-service degradation.

## Scope

| Dimension | Included | Excluded/deferred |
|---|---|---|
| Breadth | 60 stores, e-commerce reservation journey, core inventory interactions | home delivery and supplier replenishment |
| Depth | capabilities, process, information ownership, application services, platform qualities | detailed device selection |
| Time | pilot within nine months; two-year scale direction | detailed later-region schedules |
| Domains | Business, Data, Application, enabling Technology | unrelated corporate functions |

## Principles

1. **Customer promise is measurable:** reservation decisions use governed availability semantics and service objectives.
2. **Federate behind contracts:** regions can vary local implementation when common information/interface contracts and controls are met.
3. **Privacy by design:** minimize reservation data and trace approved uses.

## Risks and assumptions

- Assumption: current point-of-sale systems can publish stock changes; validate via a two-region spike.
- Risk: store workload reduces adoption; owner is COO; mitigate through co-design, pilot staffing, and measured feedback.
- Risk: inventory semantics differ; owner is data executive; mitigate with canonical definitions, stewardship, and quality rules.

## Authorization gate

Proceed when sponsor accepts scope/value, regions accept decision rights, security agrees the assurance approach, and the technical spike supports the time assumption. Otherwise revise scope or roadmap rather than hiding uncertainty.
