# P3 — Phase A: the starting point

## Outcomes

Establish a credible Architecture Vision, scope the engagement, confirm stakeholders and constraints, and secure authorization.

> **See it in practice:** Turn “launch this year” into a [decision-ready Architecture Vision](../examples/practitioner/02-phase-a-vision.md).

Phase A converts a trigger into an agreed architecture engagement. It confirms business goals/drivers, scope, stakeholders/concerns, capability/readiness, principles, risks, value proposition, and high-level Baseline/Target ideas. It produces enough clarity for commitment—not detailed domain architectures.

## Scope dimensions

| Dimension | Decision example |
|---|---|
| Breadth | Retail operations in 60 pilot stores |
| Depth | Capability and key information/application interactions, not device design |
| Time | Nine-month first transition; two-year target |
| Domains | Business, Data, Application, and enabling Technology |

## Value and risk

Express value as measurable outcomes with assumptions: “Reduce cancelled collections from 12% to below 4% while maintaining store labor cost per order.” Record uncertainty, owner, response, and trigger. An attractive slide is not a business case.

## Exercise

## Check

**Scenario:** Executives demand a vendor choice before scope and concerns are agreed. Best response? **Answer:** rapidly frame vision, outcomes, scope, stakeholders, constraints, and approval; treat vendor selection as a later evidence-based decision unless an explicit constraint makes it part of context.
