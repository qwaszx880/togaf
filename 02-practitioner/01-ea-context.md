# P1 — Context for Enterprise Architecture

## Outcomes

Assess context, configure an EA capability, and tailor engagement to purpose rather than mechanically executing phases.

> **See it in practice:** [Recover a stalled architecture initiative](../examples/practitioner/01-context-and-stakeholders.md) by fixing context, capability, and engagement together.

Start with drivers, desired outcomes, enterprise boundaries, culture, governance, maturity, delivery model, regulatory context, and existing assets. These determine method depth, content, cadence, and decision rights.

| Context signal | Sensible adaptation | Risk to guard against |
|---|---|---|
| Low maturity | Simple vocabulary, coaching, visible decisions | Creating a document bureaucracy |
| Federated organization | Partition ownership; common principles/interfaces | Local optimization |
| Product delivery | Continuous roadmaps and guardrails | Architecture detached from backlog |
| Strong regulation | Evidence, traceability, formal assurance | Slow decisions without risk basis |

## Capability checklist

Purpose and mandate; sponsor; roles and skills; governance bodies; processes integrated with portfolio/delivery; information repository; measures; improvement loop. Capability is more than an architecture team.

## Exercise

Northstar has autonomous regions, duplicated applications, and an urgent nine-month outcome. Propose: architecture scope, two federation rules, governance cadence, and three success measures. Explain why a centralized “approve everything” board would be weak.

## Check

**Scenario:** A new EA team creates forty mandatory templates before interviewing sponsors. Best correction? **Answer:** establish purpose and stakeholder outcomes, assess current capability, then tailor minimum useful content and governance. Templates follow need.
