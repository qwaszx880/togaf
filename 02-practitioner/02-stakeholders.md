# P2 — Stakeholder management

## Outcomes

Identify stakeholders, analyze power/interest, uncover concerns, and create adaptive communication.

> **See it in practice:** Build an evidence-based engagement plan while [recovering Northstar's stalled initiative](../examples/practitioner/01-context-and-stakeholders.md).

## Practical workflow

1. Identify people or groups who decide, fund, use, deliver, operate, regulate, or experience impact.
2. Elicit concerns and success measures—do not infer solely from job titles.
3. Assess influence, interest, impact, attitude, and relationships.
4. Select viewpoints and engagement modes that address concerns.
5. Track commitments and revisit the map when context changes.

| Northstar stakeholder | Likely concern | Useful representation | Engagement |
|---|---|---|---|
| COO | fulfillment time and operating cost | value stream plus outcome measures | decision workshops |
| Store manager | staffing and exception handling | process view and pilot walkthrough | co-design/pilot feedback |
| CISO | identity, privacy, abuse | risk/control view | assurance checkpoints |
| Product team | clear guardrails and dependencies | capability roadmap/API standards | continuous working sessions |

Do not confuse a high-power stakeholder with automatic architecture authority. Governance defines rights; analysis informs engagement.

## Exercise

Create a power/interest map for the case. For one resistant store manager, write a concern statement, evidence you need, appropriate viewpoint, message, channel, owner, and feedback measure.

## Check

**Scenario:** The sponsor supports the program, but users resist. Best response? **Answer:** analyze user impacts and concerns, involve representatives, communicate through suitable views, and feed validated requirements/readiness actions into the ADM—not merely escalate resistance.
