# Study plans

Times are guidelines. Add time when the diagnostic exposes a weak area.

## Six-week balanced plan (recommended)

| Week | Focus | Hands-on evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Foundation concepts; ADM purpose and cycle | Explain ADM from memory; vocabulary cards |
| 2 | ADM phases; techniques; applying ADM | Phase-purpose matrix; one iteration decision |
| 3 | Governance; content; Foundation review | Governance RACI; timed Foundation mock |
| 4 | Practitioner context, stakeholders, Phase A | Stakeholder map; Architecture Vision outline |
| 5 | Phases B–H and Requirements Management | Gap list, roadmap, compliance review |
| 6 | Supporting work, full case, scenario drills | Two timed scenario mocks plus error review |

## Four-week accelerated plan

Study 60–90 minutes on five weekdays and 2–3 hours on one weekend day.

1. **Week 1:** Foundation Modules 1–3; daily closed-book recall.
2. **Week 2:** Foundation Modules 4–6 and mock; remediate every uncertain answer.
3. **Week 3:** Practitioner Modules 1–4; complete Northstar artifacts.
4. **Week 4:** Practitioner Modules 5–8; two timed mocks and reference-navigation drills.

## Two-week intensive plan

Use only if you already practice architecture. Study 3–4 focused hours daily: morning concept review, afternoon artifact exercise, evening retrieval quiz. Days 1–4 cover Foundation; Day 5 is mock/remediation; Days 6–11 cover Practitioner; Days 12–13 are scenario simulations; Day 14 is light recall and logistics. Do not replace sleep with a final cram.

## Spaced review pattern

Review a module after **1 day, 3 days, 7 days, and 14 days**. On each review, answer from memory:

1. What problem does this concept solve?
2. Where does it connect to the ADM?
3. What output or decision does it enable?
4. What tempting misconception would produce a wrong answer?
